How lifts & elevators work

A plain-English guide to elevator technology, history, sizing and the questions buyers ask most.

Technology

Four ways an elevator moves

Traction (geared & gearless)

Steel ropes or belts run over a motor-driven sheave. A counterweight balances the cabin, so the motor only lifts the difference. Gearless versions are quieter and faster, suited to mid and high-rise buildings.

  • Best for 6+ floors and high speeds
  • Counterweight saves energy
  • Smooth, quiet ride

Machine-Room-Less (MRL)

A compact gearless motor sits inside the shaft, removing the separate machine room. This saves roughly a floor's worth of space and construction cost, a popular choice in Indian apartments and villas.

  • No machine room needed
  • Lower construction cost
  • Efficient permanent-magnet motor

Hydraulic

A piston pushed by pressurised oil raises the cabin from below. Hydraulic lifts suit low-rise buildings and heavy loads, such as goods and car lifts, but travel slower and use more energy.

  • Ideal up to about 5-6 floors
  • Handles very heavy loads
  • Simple, robust mechanism

Pneumatic (vacuum)

Air pressure difference moves a sealed cylindrical cabin up and down a tube. It needs no pit, ropes or machine room, making it a striking option for compact home installations.

  • Very compact footprint
  • Stylish transparent cabin
  • Home use, small capacity

FAQ

Questions we often hear

How much space does a home lift need?

A compact 4-person home lift typically needs a shaft of about 1.2 m x 1.4 m. Exact dimensions depend on the model and door type, so we recommend a free site survey.

How long does installation take?

For a standard residential lift, installation commonly takes two to four weeks once the shaft is ready. Larger or custom lifts take longer.

Does the lift work during a power cut?

Yes, our Automatic Rescue Device (ARD) brings the cabin to the nearest floor and opens the doors safely. Optional backup power can keep it running.

How often should a lift be serviced?

We recommend a service visit at least every month under an Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC), plus a yearly safety inspection as required by your state's lift rules.

Is GST charged on lifts?

Yes, lifts are generally charged GST at 18%. Our instant quote shows the amount before and after GST.

Can the cabin be customised?

Absolutely. Choose from blue-and-white finishes, glass walls, marble floors, lighting and door styles in our customizer.

Home / Safety

Safety is built in, never added on

Layers of mechanical, electrical and digital protection, backed by scheduled maintenance.

Overspeed Governor

If the cabin ever exceeds its safe speed, the governor triggers the safety gear to grip the guide rails.

Automatic Rescue Device

During power failure the lift travels to the nearest floor and opens its doors automatically.

Door Sensors

Light curtains detect passengers and objects, reversing the doors before contact.

Overload Protection

Load sensors prevent travel when the cabin is over capacity and sound an alert.

Fireman's Mode

In a fire alarm the lift returns to the ground floor and parks with doors open for rescue teams.

Remote Monitoring

Sensors report vibration, door cycles and faults to our service desk before you notice a problem.
